Live hardware inconsistency

To dos

  • Hardware
    Restore both arms to spec What started as a single degraded joint has widened. Across the development cycle, only 4 of 12 joints still hold precision. A servo refresh on both arms — priority on the right pan / wrist-flex / wrist-roll and cooling for the left elbow — is the single largest lift for success rates.

  • Options
    Better observability at the grasp point Right now we have limited visibility into what the robot is looking at during the critical grasp instant — the difference between "policy chose the wrong target" and "servo missed the target" can be hard to separate from external cameras alone. Adding a dedicated close-in view (or on-wrist camera) at the grasp point turns a diagnostic guess into evidence, so every failure gets categorized correctly and the fixes stack in the right order.
    Tactile sensing at the fingertips The current servos give a rough load estimate, but nothing that reliably distinguishes "gripper closed on fabric" from "gripper closed on air" at the moment of grasp. A dedicated tactile sensor at each fingertip turns that guess into a signal the policy can act on. Without it, one of the leading grasp failure modes is invisible to the robot until it tries to lift and the cloth isn't there.
